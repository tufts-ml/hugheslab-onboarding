# The Fly America Act and you

Travel funded by the NSF comes with several restrictions from the [Fly America Act](https://www.gsa.gov/policy-regulations/policy/travel-management-policy-overview/fly-america-act)- namely that you must fly on a US flag carrier airline.

However, there are several exceptions to this rule, also listed at the website above.

Tufts provides a helpful [decision diagram](https://tufts.app.box.com/s/ohdlz32lcq57mc5zeyfh3znowzl6qu2w) to help understand when you can use a non-US carrier. However, it has one important omission. You are allowed to use a European carrier if you are flying to a country outside Europe but you stop in Europe.

Here is the message I sent to Tufts Post Award to get the travel approved for my Boston > Madrid > Tangier flight.

The [GSA website](https://www.gsa.gov/policy-regulations/policy/travel-management-policy-overview/fly-america-act) seems clear that travel on an EU carrier from the US to another country outside the EU is permissible if it stops in the EU.
> The agreement with the European Union permits the use of an EU air carrier for travel outside the United States. This is the only one of these four agreements that allows for an origin or destination in a third country as long as the flight stops in the EU.

Also, the GSA published a [Federal Travel Regulation bulletin](https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/FTRBulletin11-02USEUOpenSkies.PDF) that explicitly says:
>examples of permissible routes under the current U.S.-EU Open Skies Agreement: 
  A point in the United States to a point in the European Union to a further point in a foreign country
   Example: Orlando (MCO) to London (LON) to Moscow (MOW);
